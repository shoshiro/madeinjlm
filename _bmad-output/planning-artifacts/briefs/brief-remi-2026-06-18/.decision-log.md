# Decision Log — Blessings App Product Brief

> Canonical memory and audit trail for the Blessings App product brief process. Previously titled "Simcha," then "Remi."

---

## 2026-06-18 — Session opened

- **Intent:** Create
- **Source context:** CLAUDE.md idea bank entry, user args
- **Initial framing:** "Jewish lifecycle event memory platform (Hebrew + English), per-event tiered pricing. Wedge via NYC network."

## 2026-06-18 — Discovery: Pain validation

- **Decision:** Core pain is post-event memory chaos — guest photos scattered across 40 phones, blessings evaporating, 48-hour response cliff. Validated via web research.
- **Decision:** Host (parent) is the primary pain-bearer and buyer.
- **Rejected:** "Daily baby photo stream" positioning — owned by Tinybeans/FamilyAlbum. Simcha is about sacred moments, not daily moments.

## 2026-06-18 — Product shape: "The Thread" selected

- Three concepts evaluated: The Thread (sealed future messages), The Living Montage (AI narrative), The Room (live event screen).
- **Decision:** The Thread wins on stickiness (years of lock-in via sealed content), moat (switching cost from accumulated content), and emotional payload.
- **Rejected:** The Room — live photo walls exist, medium stickiness.
- **Parked:** The Living Montage — high MVP complexity (AI narrative assembly). Could be a future premium feature.

## 2026-06-18 — Guided scripts as core differentiator

- **Decision:** The guided blessing script IS the product. It transforms casual "congrats!" into sacred blessings. Relationship-aware (grandparent vs. friend), event-specific, bilingual Hebrew/English.
- **Insight:** Digitizes existing Jewish cultural behavior (ברכות / blessings) rather than inventing new behavior. Low adoption friction.

## 2026-06-18 — Personalized recipients + baby birth as entry point

- **Decision:** Messages are personalized to specific recipients (mom, dad, grandma, the baby). Not just "to the event" but "to a person at a future moment."
- **Decision:** Baby birth is the ideal entry event — earliest lifecycle moment, maximum emotion, captures family at the start of The Thread.
- **Decision:** Include non-traditional lifecycle milestones (first day of school, birthdays, graduation) to create immediate value — not just 13-year sealed messages.

## 2026-06-18 — Revenue model: hybrid

- **Decision:** Hybrid model — freemium subscription (~₪59–79/yr) + premium event unlocks (~₪99–149 per major milestone).
- **Rationale:** Per-event is the norm for lifecycle tools ($39–$177), but subscription is proven for memory apps ($30–$110/yr). StoryWorth at $59–$199/yr is the closest pricing comp.
- **Open question:** Exact price points need validation through early adopter conversations.

## 2026-06-18 — Trust and data ownership

- **Decision:** Downloadable artifact (zip export: videos, messages, PDF timeline) available at any time. Families always own a copy.
- **Rationale:** 13-year trust horizon requires families to feel they're not hostage to the platform. Platform value is in the experience (unsealing, narrating, merging), not in holding content.

## 2026-06-18 — Thread merge at marriage

- **Decision:** When two families' children marry, their Threads can merge. Each family brings their side. Premium feature, not MVP.
- **Open question:** Permission model, billing ownership of merged Thread.

## 2026-06-18 — Competitive landscape validated

- **Finding:** No direct competitor exists. Market is fragmented across baby apps, event photo collectors, tribute platforms, time capsules, and Jewish event tools.
- **Finding:** "Sharing Simcha" (Australian) is the only Jewish-specific photo collection tool — very small, no blessing/memory layer.
- **Finding:** Time capsule space is commoditized ($2–$36/yr) with zero emotional scaffolding. This is the gap.
- **Finding:** StoryWorth ($59–$199/yr) and Remento ($99/yr) prove families pay for memory preservation subscriptions.

## 2026-06-18 — Strategy pivot: universal product, focused GTM

- **Decision:** Product engine is milestone-agnostic / universal. Marketing and launch go deep on Jewish lifecycle events (founder's NYC network = wedge). Win the wedge, then widen.
- **Decision:** Expand event types well beyond Jewish lifecycle — graduation, birthdays, army enlistment (גיוס), etc. Israeli army enlistment flagged as a high-emotion, zero-competition use case.
- **Rationale:** Product mechanics have nothing inherently Jewish; the cultural layer lives in the scripts (swappable). Broader TAM without losing the wedge.

## 2026-06-18 — Core mechanic upgrade: the Remi interviewer

- **Decision:** Replace freeform "record a blessing" with an interview flow. A persona ("Remi") guides each contributor through sequential questions; answers are stitched into a structured ~90s blessing. Solves "what do I say?" and produces far richer content.
- **Decision (MVP):** Sequential one-question-at-a-time prompts + simple clip concatenation with title cards. No AI editing required.
- **Decision (v2):** Conversational AI voice interviewer + automated video editing (ElevenLabs Hebrew/English voice). Deferred — heavy build; sequential flow delivers ~80% of value now.
- **Decision:** Store every answer as a structured clip tagged by question/contributor/relationship/milestone. Foundation for the longitudinal montage ("watch your mother answer the same question across 5 milestones"). The montage render is a v2 feature enabled by this MVP data model.
- **Rationale:** Matches founder constraints (solo, ~5 hrs/week, bias to shipping). The interview craft + persona become a compounding moat.

## 2026-06-18 — Name decision: "Remi" (was "Simcha")

- **Decision:** Project renamed from working title "Simcha" to **Remi**.
- **Rationale:** (1) "Simcha" is already taken — Simcha AI therapy app, Simcha Therapeutics, active trademark filing, plus competitor "Sharing Simcha." (2) "Remi" = short for *remember* — meaning is built in. (3) Cute, warm, works cleanly in both English and Hebrew. (4) Doubles as the AI-interviewer persona — "Remi will ask you a few questions."
- **Names checked and rejected during exploration:**
  - **Bless** — saturated (5+ Christian apps, gaming franchise, crypto network, all domains gone).
  - **Evermore** — evermore.com is a ~$300K theme-park asset; near-twin competitor "Evermore Memories" already in app stores; Taylor Swift album owns SEO; litigious trademark holder.
  - **Memoria** — memoria.com is a deathcare/memorial business (bereavement lane to avoid); identical-name app already in both stores; funded deathcare startup owns the brand; legally generic/unenforceable; Cannes-winning film owns SEO.
  - **Amber / Halcyon** — rejected by founder: don't sound good in Hebrew.
  - **Too-Hebrew options** (Tamid, Rega, Masa, Hed, etc.) — rejected by founder as leaning too Hebrew for a universal product.
- **Open item:** Run availability + trademark check on "Remi" (domain, app stores, USPTO) before committing hard. "Remi" is a common given name → expect a prefixed domain (e.g., getremi / tryremi / remi.app variants).

## 2026-06-18 — Adversarial review detail (12 findings)

Original adversarial review findings, all addressed with structural changes to the brief:

1. **Sealed content was unvalidated core bet → Fixed.** Flipped the hierarchy. Primary value is now immediate guided blessing collection. Sealed content is a bonus retention layer, not the pitch. Product works at one event with no waiting.

2. **Year 1 criteria contradicted core value → Fixed.** Success criteria now measure immediate value: paid milestones, blessings collected, host satisfaction, repeat use — not unsealing events that can't happen yet.

3. **60% contribution rate was fantasy → Fixed.** Dropped to quality-over-quantity design. Product designed to feel rich with 5–8 heartfelt blessings from "key contributors," not 40 shallow messages. No contribution rate target — measuring blessing quality (>30 seconds video) instead.

4. **Hebrew-first was a constraint, not a moat → Fixed.** Switched to English-first, Hebrew-supported. Launch market is NYC diaspora Jews ($, warm network, English-speaking). Israel is phase 2. Honest TAM: 500K Jewish households in NYC metro; 0.1% = 500 families for validation.

5. **Pricing tension between subscription and per-event → Fixed.** Killed annual subscription. Per-milestone pricing ($29/$79/$149) matches usage pattern. No empty subscription years. Subscription revisited only if repeat usage proves strong.

6. **Mobile web video recording unreliable → Fixed.** Removed in-browser recording from MVP. Primary flow: record with native camera, upload file. Fallbacks: voice recording, text. No reliance on browser media APIs.

7. **Downloadable export undermined business → Fixed.** Full export restricted to paid tiers. Clarified platform value beyond raw files: guided script context shown alongside blessings, timeline narrative, contributor attribution, unsealing ceremony. A folder of unnamed MP4s ≠ Simcha.

8. **Persona was narrative, not validated → Fixed.** Replaced fictional persona with testable hypotheses and a concrete validation plan: 10 interviews with parents from NYC network who hosted a lifecycle event in last 12 months.

9. **Vision contradicted positioning → Fixed.** Removed synagogue partnerships (B2B, wrong for 5hrs/week). Removed "universal milestones" expansion (kills positioning). Vision stays Jewish. Growth via diaspora communities, deeper lifecycle coverage, AI highlight reels.

10. **Thread merge was architecturally naive → Fixed.** Removed from product narrative and pitch. Parked in addendum as long-term vision note only. MVP and Year 1–2 don't reference it.

11. **No dark path analysis → Fixed.** Added "Handling the Hard Paths" section: divorce (both parents retain access, immutable contributions), contributor death (core value — voices outlive people), child death (memorial mode, no automated notifications), dormancy (per-milestone pricing means nothing to lapse), platform survival (export as insurance).

12. **"No direct competitor" was misleading → Fixed.** Reframed competitive positioning around user behaviors (doing nothing, WhatsApp groups, hired photographers, Tribute.co) instead of product categories. Addresses why a user would switch from what they already do.

## 2026-06-19 — Name decision: "Blessings App" (working title)

- **Decision:** Parked naming. Using "Blessings App" as a working placeholder. Final brand name TBD.
- **Rationale:** Extensive naming exploration (50+ candidates across 6+ rounds) showed that evocative single English words are almost always taken in the app/memory space. Rather than force a name, ship the product and let the name emerge from the experience.
- **Names explored and rejected (2026-06-19 session):**
  - **Remi** — blocked. remi.app is a near-identical product (AI memory storytelling, Brightful Corp). Small/pre-launch but direct collision.
  - **Roni** — clean and available, but didn't feel right emotionally.
  - **Someday** — user loved it emotionally ("Someday, they'll hear this") but App Store has "Someday - Diary App" (family memory/date tracker), and all domains taken.
  - **Spark** — heavily saturated. "Spark - Goal Tracker" is in same space. Spark Mail, Apache Spark, Spark Ring, etc.
  - **Flame** — available in the product space but feels intense/hot rather than tender/nostalgic. Doesn't work as interviewer persona.
  - **Everly** — blocked. "Everly App" and "Talk With Everly" are both active family memory products.
  - **Locket, Kept, Heirloom, Fondly, Dear, Hush, Mosaic, Sela** — rejected by founder.
  - **Forevly, Somewhen, Tendara, Remembra, Nearli, Belove, Vowen, Wellsaid, Murmur, Afterglow, Yearnly** — coined options rejected by founder.
- **Open item:** Revisit naming once the product has shape and users can react to it.
